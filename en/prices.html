What makes up the price?

The main factors behind the final price:

📏

Area

The ceiling area or partition length sets how much board and frame is needed.

🧱

Board type

Standard, moisture-resistant, fire-rated or acoustic – each has its own price.

🎨

Design

A flat ceiling costs less than a multi-level design.

💡

Lights & openings

Number of spotlights, cove lighting and AC openings.

🔩

Metal frame

Steel gauge, spacing and the hanging method.

📍

Location & height

Area, floor and ceiling height affect transport and scaffolding.

Gypsum board types – cheapest to most expensive

💰 = budget · 💰💰💰💰 = premium

1

Standard board (white)

Best for: Dry bedrooms, lounges and corridors.

Cheapest and most common. Not for wet areas.

💰 Budget
2

Moisture-resistant board (green)

Best for: Kitchens, bathrooms and laundry rooms.

A little more than standard; resists swelling and mould.

💰💰 Mid-range
3

Fire-rated board (pink/red)

Best for: Open kitchens, electrical rooms, stairwells and offices.

Slows fire spread; sometimes required in commercial buildings.

💰💰💰 Higher
4

High-density (impact-resistant) board

Best for: Busy corridors, schools and walls that take knocks.

Stronger and heavier than standard; resists dents.

💰💰💰 Higher
5

Acoustic (perforated) board

Best for: Meeting rooms, home cinemas and restaurants.

The most expensive; absorbs echo and improves sound.

💰💰💰💰 Premium

Ceiling styles – cheapest to most expensive

The same room can cost very different amounts depending on the ceiling style:

1

60×60 grid tiles

Best for: Offices, shops and stores.

Cheapest, with easy access to services.

💰 Budget
2

Flat gypsum ceiling

Best for: Bedrooms and corridors.

The most affordable clean gypsum look.

💰 Budget
3

One level with cove light

Best for: Lounges and bedrooms.

Most popular; a good balance of price and look.

💰💰 Mid-range
4

Multi-level & LED lines

Best for: Large lounges and modern offices.

More materials, lighting and time.

💰💰💰 Higher
5

Classic with cornices & mouldings

Best for: Formal sitting rooms.

Handwork and detail.

💰💰💰 Higher
6

Coffered, wooden, or glass & skylight

Best for: Villas and grand entrances.

Most expensive because of materials and precision.

💰💰💰💰 Premium

Partition types – cheapest to most expensive

1

Single board each side

Best for: Simple dividers and storage rooms.

Cheapest and quickest; limited sound insulation.

💰 Budget
2

Double board each side

Best for: Bedrooms and offices.

Stronger, quieter, and can hold shelves and a TV.

💰💰 Mid-range
3

With rock-wool insulation

Best for: Meeting rooms, clinics and kids' rooms.

Excellent sound and heat insulation.

💰💰💰 Higher
4

With glass sections

Best for: Modern offices and reception areas.

Most expensive because of glass and frames, but lets light in.

💰💰💰💰 Premium

Examples of how we price

These show how we think about price; they are not fixed quotes:

  • Flat bedroom ceiling: area in square metres × rate including frame, plus the number of light points.
  • Lounge with cove light: flat-ceiling rate + length of the cove + LED strips.
  • Office partition: length × height, plus a door and sound insulation if needed.
  • TV wall: by wall width plus the number of shelves, niches and lights.

Tips to save money

✅

Pick the right board for the room

You don't need moisture-resistant board in a dry bedroom.

✅

Do several rooms together

One bigger job lowers transport and setup costs.

✅

Cove light in the lounge only

Use a simple flat ceiling in the other rooms.

✅

Send clear photos

Clear photos and sizes get you a more accurate price from the start.
